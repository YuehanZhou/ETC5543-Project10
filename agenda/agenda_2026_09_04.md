# ETC5543 — Urban Sprawl and Biodiversity

## Meeting Agenda

**Date:** 4 September 2026

### 1. Progress since last meeting

- Additional spatial datasets have been sourced, including PSP boundaries, Victorian Land Cover Time Series, Habitat Value, BCS Conservation Areas, Urban Growth Boundary and planning overlays.
- The current study area includes Casey, Hume, Melton, Whittlesea and Wyndham.
- MSA precinct attributes were matched to PSP spatial boundaries. Of 122 MSA records, 117 were successfully matched, while 5 remain unmatched.
- The supplied VBA threatened-fauna dataset was explored and found to have limited records and uneven spatial coverage.
- Larger ALA datasets were examined for four candidate threatened species: Golden Sun Moth, Growling Grass Frog, Southern Brown Bandicoot and Striped Legless Lizard.
- Golden Sun Moth and Growling Grass Frog currently appear to be the strongest candidate species based on record availability and spatial coverage.
- Habitat Value data have been cropped and masked to the five study LGAs for initial exploration.
- Victorian Land Cover Time Series data from 1985–90 to 2015–19 have been processed using Built Environment and Urban Area classes as an initial measure of developed land.
- Urban land increased across all five study LGAs. Casey showed the largest absolute increase, while Melton showed the largest proportional increase.

### 2. Topics for discussion

#### Focal species selection

- Are Golden Sun Moth and Growling Grass Frog appropriate focal species for the project?

#### Spatial unit and development measure

- Is combining Land Cover classes 1 (Built Environment) and 2 (Urban Area) an appropriate initial measure of urban development?
- Is it acceptable to proceed with the 117 matched PSP/MSA precincts and keep the 5 unmatched records separate?
- Should PSP/MSA precincts remain our primary spatial unit, with a regular grid used only if needed?

#### Biodiversity and planning data

- How should Habitat Value be interpreted alongside threatened-species occurrence data?
- Is it appropriate to combine species occurrence, Habitat Value and BCS Conservation Areas to identify biodiversity-sensitive areas?
- How should the Urban Growth Boundary and planning overlays, particularly Environmental Significance Overlays, be incorporated into the analysis?

### 3. Next steps

- If confirmed, calculate urban expansion at PSP/MSA precinct level.
- Summarise Habitat Value at PSP/MSA level.
- Finalise focal species selection after considering ecological relevance and spatial data quality.
- Assign threatened-species occurrence records to PSP/MSA precincts.
- Integrate Habitat Value and BCS Conservation Areas with development-pressure measures.
- Examine Urban Growth Boundary and relevant planning overlays.
- Identify areas where high development pressure overlaps with high biodiversity value.
- Develop initial maps and visualisations for the presentation.

## Supervisor Feedback / Decisions

- Golden Sun Moth and Growling Grass Frog were confirmed as the two focal species for the project.

- Combining Land Cover classes 1 (Built Environment) and 2 (Urban Area) was considered appropriate as an initial measure of urban development.

- PSP/MSA precincts were confirmed as the primary spatial unit for the analysis.

- It is acceptable to proceed with the successfully matched PSP/MSA precincts and treat the unmatched records separately.

- Minh did not suggest a specific method for using Habitat Value, BCS Conservation Areas, the Urban Growth Boundary (UGB), and Environmental Significance Overlays (ESO) separately.

- Habitat Value and BCS Conservation Areas were identified as potentially useful for the biodiversity side of the analysis.

- The UGB can be used as planning and growth-boundary context.

- ESO can be considered as an additional planning overlay if it contributes useful information, but it is not essential.

- Datasets should not be forced into a single combined dataset if they do not align properly. Some spatial layers can remain separate and be used to support the interpretation of the analysis.
