# ETC5543 — Urban Sprawl and Biodiversity

## Meeting Agenda

**Date:** 28 August 2026

### 1. Progress since last meeting

- Continued using the current working research question:

  **Where is urban expansion creating the greatest pressure on threatened-fauna habitat connectivity across Melbourne's fringe growth corridors, and which areas should be prioritised for ecological protection?**

- Reviewed and checked the datasets currently available in R.

- Existing datasets include:
  - ABS population data for Casey, Hume, Melton, Whittlesea and Wyndham
  - ABS population components data
  - LGA 2025 spatial boundaries
  - MSA / PSP precinct attributes
  - PSP spatial boundaries
  - VBA threatened fauna and flora records
  - Key Biodiversity Area attributes

- Additional spatial datasets sourced this week:
  - Victorian Land Cover Time Series
  - Habitat Value
  - BCS Conservation Areas
  - Urban Growth Boundary
  - Planning Scheme Overlay polygons
  
- Initial data checks identified several issues that may affect the analysis:
  - The VBA fauna dataset contains 141 records across 28 threatened species, but record counts vary substantially between species.
  - Fauna records are unevenly distributed over time, with a large proportion concentrated in 2017–2019.
  - The flora dataset also contains relatively few records for many individual species and substantial missingness in abundance-related variables.
  - The MSA attribute table contains 122 records, while the downloaded PSP boundary layer contains 119 spatial features.
  - After standardising two obvious PSP naming differences, 117 MSA records were successfully matched to PSP geometry, while five MSA records remain unmatched.
  - Some PSPs cross multiple local government boundaries.

- Following previous supervisor advice, MSA / PSP precincts are being treated as the primary unit of analysis, with a regular or hexagonal grid considered as a possible secondary or sensitivity analysis.

- A master spatial dataset has not yet been created. The current focus is on checking compatibility, coverage and limitations of the available datasets before combining them.

### 2. Topics for discussion

#### Dataset suitability

- Do the Land Cover Time Series, Habitat Value and BCS Conservation Areas provide a suitable foundation for answering the current research question?

- Is the Land Cover Time Series an appropriate way to represent physical urban expansion, alongside population and PSP data?

- Are there any important datasets still missing before we begin the main analysis?

#### Biodiversity data

- Given the limited number of records for many threatened fauna species, should additional VBA / ALA occurrence records be sourced before selecting focal species?
- Should focal species only be selected after assessing the spatial distribution and sample size of additional occurrence records?
- Would the current fauna data be better used initially to describe threatened-fauna occurrence locations rather than temporal abundance or population change?

#### Combining the datasets

- We have not created the master dataset yet.

- Which datasets should realistically be included in the first version of the master dataset?

#### First stage of analysis

- Which analyses should be prioritised first:
  - land-cover change within or around PSP precincts;
  - Habitat Value distribution;
  - overlap with BCS Conservation Areas;
  - threatened-fauna occurrence distribution;
  - population growth as background development pressure;
  - simple spatial overlays between these layers?

- At what stage would it be appropriate to introduce road-network data or more detailed ecological-connectivity analysis?
